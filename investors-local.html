Buyers Living in Morocco

The right property,
at the right price.

Sound familiar?

Buying a property seems simple, until you actually start the process.

You have an idea of your budget, a few neighbourhoods that appeal to you and the wish to become an owner or to invest. You live here, you know the city, and you think it will be enough to look through the listings, visit a few properties and choose the one you like. Yet between the first search and the handover of the keys, every stage brings its own questions, checks and decisions. This is what the journey often looks like.

  1. 01

    It all starts with the listings.

    Like many buyers, you begin by scrolling through listings, saving the ones you like and booking a few appointments. You already know whether you want to live in the property or rent it out, but a more important question often remains unanswered: for how long? Is it a home for life, or a first purchase that you will sell in five, ten or fifteen years, when the family grows? The answer changes the neighbourhood to favour, the type of property, the size and, above all, how easily it can be sold or rented out when the time comes. Without a market study, it is also difficult to know which neighbourhoods match both your budget and this plan, which ones are developing and which ones are standing still.

    Without a strategy defined from the start, every listing looks like an opportunity.

  2. 02

    Real detective work.

    During the first weeks, you spend your evenings and weekends searching: you go through listings, call sellers and visit sales offices. You quickly notice that listings rarely give all the information you need. A price “from” applies only to the smallest unit in the building, the photos do not always match the property, some properties are already sold while the listing stays online, and the “high standing” or the “strategic location” does not always survive the visit. When you call to ask for the price, the address or a few details, the answer is often the same: “You have to come and see.”

    Prices also vary considerably, sometimes within the same street. Without a public database of real sale prices, you have to make more visits, question professionals and cross-check the information yourself to understand the market.

    What seemed like a simple search becomes real fieldwork.

  3. 03

    One property, several intermediaries.

    As your search goes on, you find the same property in several listings, sometimes at different prices. Each intermediary claims to have the owner’s direct contact and wants to earn a commission. You no longer really know who represents the seller, what the original asking price was, or how many people stand between you and the owner.

    It is hard to know who you are really dealing with, and what you will pay in the end.

  4. 04

    What the visit does not show.

    Some visits finally catch your attention. On site, you mainly notice the space, the light and the finishes. But the insulation, the waterproofing, the quality of the installations or the condition of the shared areas cannot be seen at first glance. Which documents should you ask for, which defects should you look for, and how can you check a developer’s reliability, previous projects and record of delivering on time? This information is not in any brochure.

    A good visit is not enough to judge the real quality of a property. You also need to know what to check, and how.

  5. 05

    How do you choose between your best options?

    After several weeks, you have three properties left: one is larger, another is better located, and the third is cheaper. Which one best fits your plan, and which one will hold its value best in five or ten years? Every salesperson assures you that their property is an excellent investment, with attractive rents and strong capital growth, but none of them shows you the figures that prove it.

    And while you try to compare, the pressure builds: “There is only one left”, “Another buyer is interested”, “The price goes up next week”.

    You need time to analyse your options, but you are pushed to decide before you have all the information.

  6. 06

    Now you have to negotiate.

    You have made your choice, and now you have to negotiate. But on what basis? Real transaction prices are hard to find and sellers rarely support their prices with comparisons, so it is difficult to know whether the asking price is reasonable, what offer to make and which conditions to ask for.

    Sometimes a new demand also appears along the way: the seller wants part of the price paid in cash, without it appearing in the deed of sale, and assures you that “this is how it is done”.

    You have to defend your interests in a negotiation where you do not necessarily have the same information or the same experience as the other party.

  7. 07

    Financing, in a hurry.

    You have reached an agreement, but your financing is not yet confirmed. You need to put together the bank file, gather the supporting documents, compare loan offers, calculate the purchase costs and, where it applies, prepare your Daam Sakane application. Meanwhile, the seller is waiting for your answer and may continue talking to other buyers.

    You have found the right property, but your file is not ready to buy it.

  8. 08

    And it is not over yet.

    The price is agreed and the financing is moving forward. There are still documents to check, exchanges with the notary and the bank, administrative formalities to complete, deadlines to meet and signatures to organise. And if you are buying off-plan, you will still have to follow the construction and the developer’s commitments until delivery, sometimes for several years.

You thought you were buying a property. You find yourself managing a whole real estate project.

Your questions, our answers

Where should I buy: Kénitra, Salé or Rabat?
We compare prices and demand in each city and neighbourhood based on your budget and how you will use the property.
Isn’t it cheaper to go straight to the developer?
The sales office works for the developer and only shows its own projects. With us, you compare several developers and the resale market, get an independent view of finishes and delivery, and have someone negotiating for you.
Is this price fair?
Before any offer, we compare the asking price with similar properties in the neighbourhood, based on our own research on the ground rather than on the seller’s figures.
Is the developer reliable?
We check the projects they have delivered, whether they met their deadlines and the real quality of their finishes.
How much can I borrow?
We estimate your borrowing capacity and simulate your monthly payments before you start visiting.
Am I eligible for Daam Sakane?
We check your eligibility, and the property’s based on its real sale price, before you count on it.
Is it a good investment?
We estimate the yield, the monthly income after the mortgage and the resale potential.
