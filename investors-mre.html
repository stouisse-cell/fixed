Distance should not make everything harder

Sound familiar?

You have dreamed of buying in Morocco for years. With Daam Sakane support and a country developing at full speed, investing finally seems worthwhile. Prices are rising; you feel time is running out. With a budget in mind, you start searching from abroad.

But information is scarce: no easily accessible public database of comparable sales, little market research outside the major cities, listings without prices or precise addresses. And when you ask, you keep getting the same answer that answers nothing: “You need to come and see it first.”

You send a cousin to take a look. Between work and the children, he finds a moment at the weekend and sends you a blurry video that stops just before the bathroom. “It looks good, but you need to come and see it yourself.” You have other apartments you would like him to visit, but you can tell he is short of time. You hesitate to ask for more.

Determined not to miss your chance, you book a short trip with one goal: this time, you are going to buy.

Once you arrive, the obstacles start piling up.

Read more +

You start with a development you found online, from a large, well-known developer. A polished website, a familiar name: it seems like a good place to begin. At the sales office: shutters down, dust everywhere, phone disconnected. The caretaker tells you it closed two years ago. The website is still inviting you to book an appointment.

Another viewing, another surprise: the advertised price is for the smallest apartment, on the ground floor, dark, with panoramic views of the wall opposite and a smell of mould to welcome you. The famous “starting from” price. The bright, airy apartment in the photos is available too. Just not at the same price, of course.

“Strategic location”, “luxury finishes”, “ideal as a home or investment”: everyone uses the same selling points. Based on what? The last apartment that promised all three was on the outskirts, in a budget-built block. The “luxury” stopped at the entrance hall.

The days pass in viewings, journeys across town and postponed appointments: the sales representative is held up elsewhere; the person with the keys is not answering. “Come back tomorrow.” Then the details start to blur. Which apartment had that kitchen? Was parking included? The photos are on your phone, the prices in WhatsApp, the explanations somewhere in your memory.

You go back to the apartment your cousin visited. Same seller, same floor, but the price has gone up. This time, the buyer lives abroad. The famous “MRE price”.

Getting the documents takes patience. Paying a deposit, however, apparently cannot wait. In Kénitra, there is always “a buyer from Rabat on the way with the deposit”. He never arrives during your viewing, but the moment you hesitate, he is five minutes away.

Your flight is approaching. You are torn between two properties: one larger, the other better located. You have the listing prices and the sellers’ opinions — and every seller thinks their property is a bargain. But which one actually fits your plans?

One property finally stands out. You ask about the reservation terms. That is when a large cash payment is mentioned, part of which will not appear in the deed. “It is common. Everyone does it.”

This is new to you. What does it mean for your financing, or for a future resale? You need someone to explain before you commit.

For you, it is about understanding your purchase. The seller, meanwhile, seems to wonder what you intend to do with all these answers. The explanations get shorter; the atmosphere grows tense. You ask for a night to think. The next day, you call to continue the discussion: the property is no longer available.

Did the famous buyer from Rabat finally arrive, or did your questions turn you from a “serious buyer” into a “difficult buyer”?

It is the day of your return flight. Flights, car hire, taxis, family expenses, unpaid leave: you have invested a great deal, but between conflicting information and changing prices, you still cannot make sense of the market. Discouraged, you return empty-handed, wondering whether buying back home will remain a dream, or whether it is even worth it.

Your questions, our answers

How do I choose without visiting?

We visit properties for you, film them and send you a written visit report, so you can compare at a distance.

Is the price fair?

Before any offer, we compare the asking price with similar properties in the neighbourhood, based on our own research on the ground rather than on the seller’s figures.

Will I pay a higher “MRE price”?

Not with us negotiating for you. Sellers deal with an advisor who knows the local prices, and we negotiate on the basis of that comparison.

Can I get a mortgage or Daam Sakane from abroad?

Often, yes. We check your eligibility and guide your file towards banks used to working with MRE clients. Approval remains the bank’s decision.

Is the developer reliable?

We check the projects they have delivered, whether they met their deadlines and the real quality of their finishes.

How do I sign without travelling?

We coordinate with the notary, who will advise you on a power of attorney if you cannot be there.
