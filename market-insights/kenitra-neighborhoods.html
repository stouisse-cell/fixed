Kénitra's Geography of Value

Centre Ville commands a 40–60% premium over peripheral zones. Understanding which part of the city you are buying in matters as much as the asking price per square metre.

Published
May 16, 2026
By
Rahim International
Read time
7 minutes

Most buyers come to Kénitra with a budget and a price-per-square-metre figure gathered from listings. That is the wrong starting point. The city's 12 residential zones span from roughly 5,800 MAD per square metre for older eastern stock to 15,000 MAD for premium new builds in Centre Ville — and that gap has nothing to do with the apartment itself. It reflects geography: proximity to the LGV station, employment depth, rental demand structure, and how quickly you can sell if your circumstances change. Two identically finished apartments at the same headline price can represent entirely different decisions depending on which of Kénitra's zones they sit in.

The Oued Sebou defines the northern edge. The Royal Air Force base occupies a large portion of the northwest. A military complex sits close to the urban core. The railway corridor bisects the historic fabric from the south. And with limited arterial roads connecting one side to the other, two neighbourhoods that appear adjacent on a map can be a 30-minute drive apart at peak hours. Understanding these divisions before you read any listing is the single most useful preparation a buyer can do.

Zone 01 — Centre Ville: The Pricing Anchor

Centre Ville — Maamora, Mimosas, La Ville Haute, Val Fleury — is the city's historic, civic, and commercial core. Buy prices run 7,000–15,000 MAD per square metre, with Maamora and Mimosas anchoring the premium end (10,000–15,000) and La Ville Haute offering a broader band (7,000–12,500). Long-let rents range from 2,150 to 8,500 MAD per month depending on apartment size and building quality.

The premium is earned, not aspirational. The LGV station sits inside the zone — often within walking distance — putting Rabat 14 minutes away and Casablanca under an hour. Administrative employment, banking, retail, and professional services all concentrate here. Rental demand is structural rather than cyclical, absorbing long-let tenants continuously. And Centre Ville produces Kénitra's strongest resale liquidity: the ability to exit quickly at a fair price, which for a five-to-ten-year investor matters as much as entry yield. Buyers who benchmark this zone against cheaper alternatives and conclude they are overpaying are usually making the wrong comparison. They are not buying the same product at a higher price — they are buying a different product entirely.

"The premium Centre Ville commands over the periphery reflects access, employment depth, and exit liquidity that the outer zones structurally cannot replicate."

Ali Rahim · Founder, Rahim International

Zones 02 & 03 — East-Central and Campus: Central Adjacency

East-Central Kénitra — El Mellah, Quartier Genie, La Cigogne, La Chenille, Jarda al Qadi — is the first residential ring east of the core. Buy prices run 6,500–10,000 MAD per square metre; rents 2,300–4,500 MAD per month. The LGV station is five to fifteen minutes by car depending on which street you are on. El Mellah anchors the zone with older stock and the city's main bus station alongside gradual redevelopment; the sub-neighbourhoods further east offer practical apartment stock at consistently lower prices than the core while retaining genuine central adjacency.

Campus Universitaire, separated from the centre by the railway line, operates on a different logic entirely. This is Kénitra's student rental belt, driven by sustained demand from Ibn Tofail University rather than commuter cycles. Rents run 2,500–4,500 MAD per month on small apartments with steady occupancy tied to the academic calendar. New supply is limited; this is a resale-driven, usage-led zone for rental investors who understand the product and the tenant base.

Zone 04 — Kasbah Mehdia: North-West Growth Corridor

Kasbah Mehdia has undergone one of Kénitra's most visible neighbourhood transformations over the past two decades. The zone spans a wide range: Addoha (Résidence du Golf) used apartments trade at 200,000–250,000 MAD in total price terms — the city's densest concentration of Daam Sakani Tier 1 inventory, where qualifying units at or below 300,000 MAD attract the 100,000 MAD subsidy. Alliance Mehdia has evolved considerably from its origins: new mid-range apartment developments trade at 7,400–9,000 MAD per square metre alongside a mall, expanding commercial strips, and a Villa du Golf zone at 7,500–9,500 MAD/m². Kasbah El Mehdia sits on the ancient kasbah hill, transformed since the 1990s into a mix of R+2/R+3 houses and villa areas. The LGV is 18–28 minutes by car. For first-home buyers and MRE buyers using the subsidy programme, this corridor deserves serious attention.

Zone 05 — Mehdia Beach: A Separate Market

Mehdia Beach is an entirely different market from the city behind it. New coastal apartments start from 12,000 MAD per square metre — above much of the urban market — despite sitting 25–35 minutes from the LGV station. The driver is seasonal demand and second-home positioning, not daily commuting. Long-let rents run 3,200–5,500+ MAD per month, and summer short-let rates behave very differently again. For MRE second-home buyers, short-let investors, and lifestyle buyers wanting Atlantic access within reach of Rabat, Mehdia's logic is coherent on its own terms. Comparing it against urban Kénitra pricing is like comparing a beach apartment to a city apartment — the numbers only make sense once you understand which product you are actually buying.

Zones 06 & 07 — The Southern Belts

South-Central Kénitra — Bir Rami Ouest, Bir Rami Est, Maghreb Al Arabi — forms the first coherent residential ring below the centre. Bir Rami Ouest is widely regarded as Kénitra's most prestigious residential address outside Centre Ville: larger plots, mature streets, and an established villa character built over decades. Bir Rami Est is more mixed, with newer apartments alongside older stock. Apartment buy prices across the zone run 6,500–8,800 MAD per square metre; rents 2,800–5,500 MAD per month. The LGV is 10–18 minutes away. For families who want practical centre access without paying full Centre Ville pricing, this is the natural first comparison.

South Kénitra — Bir Rami Sud, Al Hadika 1 & 2, Jenane — is the deeper southern belt, more affordability-led and further out. Jenane has developed into an active zone of R+3 and R+4 apartment buildings following the city's steady southward growth. Buy prices run 6,200–9,000 MAD per square metre; rents 2,300–4,800 MAD per month; LGV 15–24 minutes. Both southern belts carry strong Daam Sakani Tier 2 pockets, and the sustained southward growth trajectory reinforces the long-term logic of buying here.

Zones 08 & 09 — The Western Arc

West-Central Kénitra — Taibia, La Cité, Le Vallon, Quartier Sayad — runs along the Sebou side of the city. La Cité occupies a quiet riverfront position north of La Ville Haute; Le Vallon and Quartier Sayad fill the zone with mid-market apartment stock closer to the central fabric than the deeper western belt. Buy prices run 6,000–8,500 MAD per square metre; rents 2,500–4,500 MAD per month; LGV 10–20 minutes. For buyers who want west-of-centre value without moving far out, this is the zone to examine first.

West Kénitra — Oulad Oujih, Al Haouzia, Ismailia, Al Haddadda — spans a wider range than its collective label implies. Al Haouzia has undergone a genuine residential upgrade in recent years: new apartment blocks, private schools, cafés, and boutique food shops have made it one of the city's more attractive new middle-class neighbourhoods. Buy prices across the zone run 6,500–9,000 MAD per square metre; rents 2,500–5,500 MAD per month; LGV 18–30 minutes. For value-led buyers and long-let investors who do not need central prestige, the western belt consistently delivers more per square metre than the core.

Daam Sakani at a glance: Tier 1 (≤300,000 MAD, subsidy 100,000 MAD) has its strongest fit in Addoha-side Kasbah Mehdia, parts of the western and south belts, South-East Kénitra, and North Kénitra. Tier 2 (300,001–700,000 MAD, subsidy 70,000 MAD) concentrates in South-Central, South Kénitra, Alliance-side Kasbah Mehdia, and selected East Kénitra pockets around Al Montazah. Centre Ville qualifies selectively — the zone's price floor limits eligibility across most stock.

Zones 10, 11 & 12 — The Outer City

East Kénitra — Ancienne Medina (Khabazzate), Quartiers Populaires, Al Montazah, Bab Fes — tells the clearest story of how the city actually lives beyond its promotional image. Al Montazah stands apart: a newer neighbourhood with green spaces, a public garden, schools, restaurants, and cafés, trading at 8,000–9,000 MAD per square metre with long-let rents of 3,000–3,500 MAD per month. The Ancienne Medina and hay chaabi sub-neighbourhoods carry rents of 1,800–2,500 MAD per month — the floor of Kénitra's rental market — with older stock and a distinct, deeply local character. LGV access is 12–25 minutes depending on sub-neighbourhood.

South-East Kénitra — Saknia, Hay El Oufaa, Ain Sebaa — follows the road toward Rabat. Saknia was effectively the city's outer edge thirty years ago; today it is a fully integrated district with planned streets, banks, restaurants, and everyday services. Buy prices run 6,000–9,000 MAD per square metre across the zone; long-let rents 1,500–2,300 MAD per month; LGV 20–30 minutes. North Kénitra — Quartier l'Assam, Fouarate — is a lower-profile residential edge with thin pricing visibility on mainstream portals and rents of 1,800–2,500 MAD per month. The appeal, where it exists, is entirely about entry-level cost.

These outer zones serve genuine, durable housing demand from local families, civil servants, and industrial workers. But they carry meaningfully lower resale liquidity than the centre or growth corridors. Selling quickly at a fair price here is harder — a trade-off that suits long-term owner-occupiers and Daam Sakani buyers far better than investors who need a flexible exit within five years.

Kénitra rewards buyers who understand its internal geography before they open a single listing. The headline price per square metre is the beginning of the analysis. The zone it sits in, the access it provides, the demand profile it draws, and the exit it allows — those are the variables that determine whether the investment performs. Get the zone right first. The rest follows.
